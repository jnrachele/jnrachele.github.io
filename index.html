What I work on

Neighbourhoods and health inequalities

Why people in disadvantaged neighbourhoods carry a heavier burden of obesity, poor mental health and declining physical function, followed over time in cohorts such as HABITAT and HILDA.

Papers on this

Walkable, liveable cities

How walkability, public transport, green space and density shape walking, physical activity and heart health, and whether good design can narrow the gap between neighbourhoods.

Papers on this

Disability and inclusive places

Working with people with disability and local government to make public spaces, transport and information genuinely inclusive, from inner-city Melbourne to national monitoring frameworks.

I work at the intersection of urban design and health inequities, bringing together social epidemiology, disability research and geospatial data. My research asks why people living in disadvantaged neighbourhoods have poorer health, and which features of the places we build (walkable streets, public transport, green space, safe and inclusive public spaces) can help close those gaps.
